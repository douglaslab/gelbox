## Rulings

From the brief of 2026-10-12:

- One concept per stop; one to three controls at first.
- Alive and touchable first screen; first gesture immediate and reversible;
  visible handles, no hover-only interaction.
- Comparison trace (previous result or adjacent reference gel).
- Introduction, complication, twist, free play.
- Failure is content; continuous movement into and out of it.
- Presets are editable starting conditions; edits keep the experiment and
  show the changed state.
- Skip freely; optional progress markers; no mandatory unlocks, modal
  tutorials, timers or compulsory quizzes.
- Time scrubbing central; experiment time separate from ambient motion.
- One experiment state behind gel, loupe and readouts; no tutorial physics.
- Success never judged solely by matching a hidden slider value.
- Layered walkthrough, one surface for students and researchers; stops are
  simple concepts, plasmid supercoiling among them (Shawn, 2026-10-12).
- The base sandbox loads first; a button reveals the tour as an overlay;
  exercises are cards, no literal map (Shawn, 2026-10-12).
- Progress is session-only: nothing saved, no reset control (Shawn, 2026-10-12).
- Reference photographs linked, not redistributed.
- Nothing here touches the 1.1 builds or assumes the work ships in 1.1.

## Contradictions in T8, confirmed on 2026-10-12

- **Tip 3 caption versus body.** The body says TAE suits longer fragments (typically over 1 kb) and preparative work, and TBE gives better separation of small fragments and holds up in long runs. The caption says TAE is best for smaller fragments and "TBS" for heavier ones. The caption is reversed and misspelt; the body agrees with LZ and BT. Use the body.
- **Tip 4 caption versus body.** The body says Orange G migrates like a 50 bp fragment and xylene cyanol like a 4,000 bp fragment. The caption says xylene cyanol migrates like a shorter fragment and Orange G like a heavier one. The caption is reversed; the body agrees with Gelbox's dye constants (xylene cyanol 6,000 to 7,000 bp, cresol red 700 to 900, bromophenol blue 300 to 500, Orange G 50 to 100 in `dye.ts`; the xylene cyanol figure differs from the article's 4,000 bp, which is expected since apparent dye size depends on gel concentration). Use the body.

## Gelbox controls and model today

Gel: buffer preset (H2O, .5x TBE, 1x TAE) with Tris, Borate, Acetate and EDTA sliders; lanes 7 to 20; well damage 0 to 1; voltage −300 to 300 V, notch 70 V; rotation; time 0 to 180 min. Fragment: size 100 bp to 14 kb, amount 1 to 175 ng, aspect ratio 1 to 16, multimer 1 to 7, degrade 0 to 2, colour; dyes xylene cyanol, cresol red, bromophenol blue, Orange G at 0 to 125 ng. Sample buffer per tube (same four sliders).

Band position: normalised size through a curve, `curveBase + (1 − y)^3.7`, scaled by `(1 + (V − 70)/70)` and by time. `curveBase` is 0.05, or 0.07 when the gel buffer equals the 1x TAE preset exactly; any other composition, including an edited TAE, gets 0.05. The sample buffer is read only for the H2O smear-above effect. Mass does not enter position. Brightness is `(mass/175)^0.75`, reduced by degradation. Blur grows with time and is larger for small fragments. Within-band curl ("smile" in the code) is 35% of band height, ramping in over the first ten minutes, sign from voltage polarity, independent of mass and of voltage magnitude. Flames start above 140 ng (80% of 175) and reach twice the band height at 175 ng. Well damage warps the band top. There is no gel-level term, so nothing curves the row of lanes.

## Regression fixtures

Each proposed comparison is a one-variable series that becomes a saved gel (native JSON and web fixture) with expected band position, width, brightness and distortion recorded separately per lane. Native and web have separate engines and must each reproduce the fixture; the existing parity ledger under `web/docs/` is the place to add them.

## Model-upgrade scope, in the order the stops need it

1. **Loading** (case 5): mass into position and width; curl amplitude as a function of mass, zero below the onset; keep flames above 140 ng. Needed by the first stop.
2. **Buffer** (case 3): a 0.9 speed factor for TBE relative to TAE and a size-dependent resolution term; read the sample buffer; make the composition sliders continuous.
3. **Voltage** (case 7): an across-lane smile term driven by voltage magnitude and time, separate from within-band curl; voltage-dependent blur.
4. **Dyes** (case 4): supported today.
5. **Plasmid** (case 10): a topology state per species with three mobilities and a control that moves mass between them; the multimer slider already supplies the concatemer ladder. Medium change, no new gel-level control.
6. **Gel %** (case 2): a new control with the Van Winkle relation. Larger change; a later stop.

Cases 6 and 8 have no control and no cheap mechanism; they appear only as named causes in a stop's evidence drawer.

## Prompt, verbatim

Gelbox is a gel electrophoresis simulator from the Douglas Lab at UCSF, built with Chaim Gingold: a native Mac and Windows app and, since October 2026, a web beta at douglaslab.org/gelbox/app. People load samples into tubes, drag the time slider, and watch bands separate on a gel they can touch; loupes drill into a molecular view of any spot on the gel. This surface is for two people at once, a student meeting a gel for the first time and a researcher who arrived with a bad gel and wants to know why, served by one layered walkthrough whose copy is paced for the student and whose exits and evidence are there for the researcher; Shawn settled the layering on 2026-10-12. This round designs the tour: a series of explorable gel exercises, each a live preconfigured gel with one or two controls, reached from a button on the ordinary gel page, plus the way from an exercise into the full workspace and back. It is the first round for this surface.

Today there is no walkthrough. The web app opens on the gel page: seven microtubes above a black gel, lane 1 loaded with a 1 kb ladder whose bands are already separated because the time slider under the gel sits at "3:00", with a clock icon at its left end and a timer at its right. A sliders button beside the timer opens the "Gel" panel: a "Buffer" picker with "H2O", ".5x TBE" and "1x TAE", four sliders labelled "Tris", "Borate", "Acetate" and "EDTA" showing values like "44.5 mM", then icon sliders for lane count (reading "7"), well damage, voltage (reading "70 V", range minus 300 to 300 with a notch at 70) and gel rotation.
